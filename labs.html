A news story says a laboratory was found. A sentencing note says what was on the floor, what the analyst could prove, and what the judge did with it. This page collects the second kind — and marks the places where only the first kind exists. Every paragraph below is a quotation from a published judgment.

The limit of this page

The judgments say five clandestine laboratories were located. Six addresses appear across the same judgments in connection with manufacture or with the items of manufacture — Cashel Street, Main South Road, Maunsell Street, Gregory Avenue, Southey Street and the Lyttelton address. The judgments do not reconcile that difference, so this page does not either.

Where an address is named and nothing else is said about it, that is what is printed. Where the Crown could not prove the scale, that finding is printed too — it is part of the record and it is the reason some of the numbers below are smaller than the reporting around them.

What the operation was worth

from the same judgments

  • Various types of substances containing pseudoephedrine were also located at four addresses, capable of producing methamphetamine worth between $216,000 and $306,000.R v Black [2012] NZHC 2902 [4]
  • Cash worth $134,855 was also found.R v Black [2012] NZHC 2902 [4]

  • Given your limited role, it is not necessary for me to detail the facts of the methamphetamine enterprise, other than to observe that a significant quantity of methamphetamine was found at four different addresses, all linked in some way to Mr Newton, and that various types of substances containing pseudoephedrine were also located at four addresses.R v Aitken [2012] NZHC 2901 [2]
  • The evidence of your connection to the conspiracy was relatively sparse, comprising an agreement to lend Mr Newton the sum of $180,000 at an interest rate of 80% over four months.R v McKenzie [2013] NZHC 3351 [6]

The note also included reference to “Lance Lee” and to “60 x 20 Rits for you if you want”. The later cases are not lab cases

Avon · 2026

The biggest operation in the record ends in supply and organised-crime charges, not manufacture. The Avon sentencing notes describe what the police found and how many people were arrested; they describe no laboratory.

The police operation discovered the gang was transporting both methamphetamine and cocaine from the North Island based Comancheros to members here in Christchurch. Cash was then being moved back to Auckland. The seven-month operation began in February 2024 and exposed the commercial supply and distribution of what is calculated to be at least 30 kilograms of Class A controlled drugs, around two thirds of which was methamphetamine and one third, cocaine.

R v Connor [2026] NZHC 933 [2]

On termination in late August 2024, the police made 21 arrests, including the arrest of yourself.

R v Connor [2026] NZHC 933 [2]

How these extracts were made

method

Each quotation was cut from a judgment text held in this project's evidence folder and machine-checked against it: the build fails if a quoted string is not found in the source. Line breaks were normalised to single spaces; nothing else was altered, including the judgment's own spelling.

Paragraph numbers are the judgment's own. Where a judgment has a table of contents that repeats them, the number cited is the one attached to the paragraph in the body of the text.

One thing the record mentions but does not contain: the schedule of items that the sentencing judge said would be attached to his notes. The published text says the schedule is attached; the attachment is not in the published text. The item-level inventory for those addresses is therefore not in this record, and this page does not invent one.
